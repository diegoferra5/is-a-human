The story

Banks in LatAm still serve many customers by phone. Fraudsters can now dial those lines with a full synthetic stack — speech recognition, a language model, and a cloned voice. This system listens to a recorded call and answers one question: was the incoming caller a real person?

What the bank hears

Every sample is a two-sided phone call. The bank already runs an AI voice agent. A second voice — the caller — is trying to complete a customer-service script. We never try to identify the person. We only decide whether that incoming voice is human or synthetic.

Stereo call · two tracks in one file

Stereo telephony, 8 kHz, 16-bit PCM. Judges send the whole WAV as base64 to POST /detect.

Organizer hint

You get both channels for a reason

The caller track is not the only evidence. The agent’s turns tell you what the caller was reacting to — interruptions, silence, and scripted prompts. That is why we measure conversation timing, not only voice texture.

Organizer hint

Depth beats breadth

We measured 68 numeric features. Most did not hold up. Breath sounds, room echo, and pitch jitter scored near zero on this 8 kHz phone audio. The shipped model uses eight features across two heads.

How it works

Think of the system as a short assembly line. Audio goes in. Speech stretches are marked. Two specialists each give a score. A mixer combines those scores into one probability. If that probability is 0.5 or higher, we say the caller is synthetic.

The five steps, in plain language

StepWhat happensSay this to judges
1. SplitThe stereo file is pulled apart. Channel 0 is the caller. Channel 1 is the agent.Demux. We never classify the agent.
2. Find speechWe mark when each side is talking, pausing, or talking over the other.Hybrid VAD → turn ledger. Fitted energy VAD; Silero only if energy hears nothing.
3. MeasureFrom those turns we compute how messy the voice is, and how the caller waits and recovers.Acoustic + behavioural features on the VAD ledger.
4. Two opinionsA voice specialist and a conversation specialist each output P(synthetic).Two logistic-regression heads. Not a deep neural net.

Voice activity detection — finding the turns

VAD means “voice activity detection”: a detector that answers “is someone speaking right now?” Judges send raw audio, not the organizer’s turn files. If our turns do not match the recipe the features were trained on, the later scores drift.

Safety rule

Unsure disagreement

If the two heads vote opposite ways and the mixer is sitting near a coin flip (within 0.10 of 0.5), we trust the head that is farther from 0.5.

resolve_disagreement, disagree_unsure = 0.10. On val this saved one human and broke none.

Safety rule

Talkative quiet patient

A polite caller who waits about 1.4–2.0 seconds, keeps a tidy volume, and still takes many turns can look like a bot. If they have 24 or more caller turns, we override toward human.

resolve_talkative_quiet. Gate is wait + rms_cv; the expert only fires for the talkative subtype.

The two clues

Humans are messy. Machines are consistent. That is the whole idea, measured two ways: how the voice sounds, and how the caller behaves when the agent interrupts, goes quiet, or finishes a turn.

Acoustic head · 5 features

The voice itself

A real throat does not hold volume, brightness, and “buzziness” perfectly still. A vocoder often does. We do not use average loudness. Loud synthetics in this dump look like an injection artifact, not a law of nature.

Val accuracy 87.3% · AUC 0.963. Logistic regression on caller_rms_cv, caller_zcr_std, caller_crest_factor_cv, caller_spectral_flatness_std, caller_spectral_centroid_std.

Behavioural head · 3 features

The conversation

After the agent stops, a typical human in this set answers in about 1.2 seconds. A typical synthetic waits about 2.6 seconds, and does so more uniformly. Humans also leave the agent more airtime and recover less consistently after turbulence.

Val accuracy 93.0% · AUC 0.979. Features: caller_response_latency_pos_median_s, agent_talk_ratio, agent_aligned_recovery_cv. Mixer weight 2.41 vs 1.65 — timing wins ties.

Judge brief

A script you can actually say

“We classify only the incoming caller. The agent channel is context. First we run a hybrid VAD so we know who spoke when — fitted energy VAD, 98% overlap with the organizer turns, about 9 milliseconds.”

“Then two small logistic heads. Acoustic: humans are messier in loudness, brightness, and buzziness. We deliberately do not use average volume, because the synthetics in this dump are louder at a fixed digital level — that may not survive the hidden set.”

“Behavioural: after the agent yields the floor, humans come back in about 1.2 seconds, synthetics in about 2.6. The mixer learned to trust timing more than texture. If the heads disagree and the mixer is unsure, we take the sharper head.”

“Val is 69 of 71 — 97.2%. AUC 0.997. Full labeled set 96.6%. Live scoring is 22 milliseconds. Semantic is built but not fused — no transcripts, and depth beats breadth.”

Words you may need

Audio and telephony
  • Stereo — two tracks in one file. Channel 0 caller, channel 1 agent.
  • 8 kHz / PCM — phone-line quality, not studio quality. Fine breath and formant cues mostly die here.
  • Base64 — the WAV is encoded as text so it can travel in a JSON HTTP body.
  • Demux — splitting those two tracks apart.
  • TTS / vocoder — text-to-speech; the machine that “sings” the synthetic caller.
  • ASR + LLM + TTS stack — hear the agent, think of a reply, speak it. Not a replayed recording.
Detection and machine learning
  • VAD — voice activity detection; marks speech vs silence.
  • Turn ledger — the timeline of who spoke when, including overlap.
  • Feature — a single number we compute from the call (wait time, loudness variation, …).
  • Logistic regression — a simple, readable model: weighted sum of features, then a 0–1 probability. No deep net in production.
  • Head — one of those models, specialized (acoustic or behavioural).
  • Stacked fusion — a third logistic model that takes the two head probabilities as its inputs.
  • P(synthetic) — our estimated probability the caller is a machine. Verdict is yes at 0.5 or above.
  • Confidence — how sure we are of the yes/no label, not the raw probability. If we say human with P=0.2, confidence is 0.8.
How judging numbers work
  • Accuracy — share of calls we get right.
  • TPR / TNR — how often we catch synthetics, and how often we correctly clear humans.
  • AUC — ranking quality. 1.0 means every synthetic scored above every human.
  • Brier score — how honest the probabilities are, not just the yes/no. The judge can compute this from confidence.
  • Cohen’s d — how far apart the two groups sit, in standard deviations. |d| > 0.8 is large.
Why we froze
  • Speaker-disjoint — train, val, and the hidden set do not share callers. Memorizing one polite human will not transfer.
  • Overfit — a model that chases the last two val mistakes usually gets worse on new voices.
  • Injection artifact — a pattern that is true in this zip file (synths louder) but may be a side effect of how they were generated.
  • IoU — intersection-over-union. How well our speech segments cover the organizer’s segments. High IoU keeps train and serve features aligned.
