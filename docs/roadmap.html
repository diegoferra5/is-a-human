The Liveness Ladder

A bank's phone line picks up and someone starts talking. Is it a person, or a machine wearing a human voice?

The problem

Anyone can clone a voice from a few seconds of audio. For millions of people in Latin America the phone is the only way they bank, and right now nothing on that line can tell a real customer from a robot.

What we're up against isn't a recording being played down the line. It's a robot caller that listens, thinks, and speaks — improvising through a real conversation. So we're not hunting for a glitch in the audio. We're asking whether there's a mind on the line.

The idea

Two ways to catch a fake bill: feel the paper, or ask the person holding it a question. Everyone else will feel the paper. We ask the question.

The challenge names three layers you can attack this from. We go deep on two of them — and skip the obvious one, on purpose.

Layer 1 · Conversational behavior

Machines pause too well

We measure how the caller handles the flow of the call: how long they take to answer, how much that varies, and whether they ever talk over the agent.

Why it worksA robot caller is three steps in a row — hear, think, speak. It physically cannot start answering until it detects you've stopped talking, so its pause is always about the same length. A person predicts where your sentence is going and jumps in early. That gap is structural. You can't engineer it away without breaking the agent.

Agent interrupts.
Person — "perdón— perdón, dígame"
Machine — clean sentence, 1.2 s later. Every time.

Layer 2 · Semantic

Machines can't say "I don't have that"

We read what the caller actually said at the moments the agent asks about something that does not exist.

Why it worksA language model is built to produce a plausible answer, and it has no real account to check against — so it fills the gap. A person just knows they don't have a Platino account and says so. That reflex to invent belongs to the model, not to the voice, so it shows up no matter which voice engine is bolted on top.

"¿Me confirma su cuenta Platino?"
Person — "No, yo no tengo esa cuenta."
Machine — "Sí, es la 4409-8821…"

Layer 3 · Acoustic

What the voice sounds like

Train a model on the audio itself — the artefacts and fingerprints a synthetic voice leaves behind.

Why we deprioritise itEvery voice engine leaves a different fingerprint, and we're graded on engines we have never heard. A model that learns these ones learns the wrong thing. Layers 1 and 2 don't care what the voice sounds like, which is exactly why they survive a new engine. We add this last, if at all, and weight it low.

And then · the proof

We build the attacker and let it call us

Anyone can claim their detector generalises. We show it. We build our own robot caller — using a voice engine that appears nowhere in the dataset — and put it on a real phone line while the judges watch. The verdict lands while the call is still going.

Then we hand a judge the phone, they call the same number, and come back human.

Same detector underneath, just wired to a live call instead of a file. We build these agents for a living, so this is the one thing in the room nobody else can do.

The roadmap

The rule: every step ends with a working endpoint that's live and better than the last. If we run out of time anywhere, we still have something to hand in.
